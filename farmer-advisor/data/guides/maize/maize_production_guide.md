# Maize Production Guide for Smallholder Farmers in Uganda

## 1. Purpose

This guide summarises practical steps that smallholder farmers in Uganda can use to establish and manage a healthy maize crop. Recommendations can vary with maize variety, soil type, rainfall, altitude, and whether maize is grown alone or intercropped. Follow the seed supplier's instructions and seek advice from a local agricultural extension officer when conditions differ.

## 2. Choose suitable seed

- Use certified maize seed from a trusted, licensed agro-input dealer.
- Select a variety suited to the local agro-ecological conditions, rainfall pattern, maturity period, and intended use.
- Check the seed label for the variety name, maturity information, and planting instructions.
- Avoid damaged, mouldy, mixed, or poorly stored seed.
- Do not assume that one variety or seed rate is suitable for every district.

## 3. Prepare the field

- Choose a field with reasonably well-drained soil. Avoid areas where water remains standing for long periods.
- Clear heavy weeds and prepare a workable seedbed before the rains become established.
- Where possible, retain useful crop residues or use suitable organic materials to protect the soil, while avoiding practices that harbour serious pests or diseases.
- Plan rows before planting. Planting in straight rows makes weeding, crop inspection, and harvesting easier.

## 4. Plant at the right time

- Aim to plant at the onset of reliable rains, when there is enough moisture in the soil for germination.
- Avoid planting too late in the season because the crop may face moisture stress during flowering and grain filling, and late crops may experience greater pest and disease pressure.
- Do not plant into waterlogged soil.
- Place seed at a reasonably uniform depth. Ugandan extension guidance describes shallower placement in moist soil and deeper placement for dry planting; follow the guidance for the actual soil moisture and the seed variety.

## 5. Spacing and planting method

Correct spacing helps maize plants access sunlight, water, and nutrients and makes field operations easier.

Ugandan extension manuals describe more than one planting arrangement. Examples for a maize monocrop include:

- About 75 cm between rows and 25 cm between planting stations, with one seed per station.
- About 75 cm between rows and 60 cm between stations, with two seeds per station.

These are alternative arrangements, not instructions to combine. The appropriate arrangement depends on the variety, soil fertility, rainfall, planting method, and seed supplier's recommendation. If two seeds are planted at a station, follow local extension guidance on whether and when to thin.

For maize intercropped with beans or another legume, do not automatically use the monocrop layout. Use a locally recommended intercrop arrangement that gives both crops adequate space.

## 6. Manage weeds early

- Keep the field free of heavy weed competition, especially during the early growth stages.
- Inspect the field regularly and remove weeds while they are still small.
- Weed again when necessary so that weeds do not compete with maize for water, nutrients, and light.
- Hand hoeing is an option for small fields. Take care not to cut maize roots or bury young plants.
- If considering herbicides, use only a product registered for the intended crop and situation. Read and follow the label, wear the required protective equipment, and ask a qualified extension officer or agrochemical professional for guidance. This guide does not prescribe herbicide products or doses.

## 7. Maintain soil fertility

- Where possible, use a soil test to understand the field's nutrient status and soil pH before deciding on fertiliser or lime.
- Organic materials such as well-decomposed manure or compost can contribute organic matter and nutrients. Use mature material and distribute it evenly.
- Maize requires balanced nutrition. The right fertiliser type, rate, timing, and placement depend on the soil test, crop condition, product formulation, and local recommendations.
- Do not place fertiliser directly against the seed. Keep fertiliser and seed separated by soil.
- Follow the fertiliser label and advice from an agricultural extension officer. Avoid applying an unverified rate based only on a general guide.

## 8. Monitor crop health and pests

Inspect the field at least once a week, and more often when pest damage is observed.

Look for:
- Small holes or window-like feeding marks on young leaves.
- Caterpillars or frass (insect droppings) in the central leaf funnel, which may indicate fall armyworm.
- Dead central shoots, damaged stems, or weak plants.
- Discoloured leaves, unusual spots, rotting, or plants that are wilting.

What to do:
- Check several parts of the field rather than judging the whole crop from one damaged plant.
- Remove and inspect affected plants where practical, and record where damage is increasing.
- Ask a local extension officer to help identify serious or spreading problems.
- Do not apply pesticides based only on a photograph or an uncertain diagnosis. Never guess a pesticide dose; follow the registered product label and qualified advice.

## 9. Water and soil conservation

- Plant when there is adequate soil moisture and avoid planting in waterlogged areas.
- On sloping land, use locally suitable soil and water conservation practices, such as contour-aligned rows or grass strips, where recommended.
- Mulch or retain suitable crop residues where practical to reduce soil exposure and moisture loss.
- Keep drainage channels functional where waterlogging is a risk, without causing erosion downstream.

## 10. Harvesting and post-harvest handling

- Harvest when the cobs and grain have matured and the crop has dried sufficiently for harvest.
- Avoid leaving mature maize exposed in the field longer than necessary, especially where pests, mould, theft, or weather damage are risks.
- Dry harvested cobs or grain thoroughly on a clean surface and protect them from rain, animals, and contamination.
- Store only dry grain in a clean, dry, well-maintained storage area or suitable storage container.
- Inspect stored grain regularly for insects, mould, leaks, and signs of moisture. Keep storage areas clean and prevent re-wetting.

## 11. Keep simple farm records

Record:
- The maize variety and seed source.
- Planting date and field size.
- Spacing and seed quantity used.
- Manure and fertiliser products applied, including amounts and dates.
- Weeding dates and pest or disease observations.
- Harvest date and quantity harvested.
- Storage losses or problems.

Records help compare seasons and identify which practices are working on the farm.

## 12. Important advice for farmers

There is no single fertiliser rate, variety, planting date, or pest-control treatment that is correct for every farm in Uganda. Soil, rainfall, variety, and field history matter. Use soil-test results and local extension advice for decisions involving fertiliser, lime, pesticides, and serious crop problems.
